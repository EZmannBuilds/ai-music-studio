# Qanun: research records

What was read, what was not, and the limits of each claim the guide page makes. The page carries
the conclusions; this file carries the evidence.

## Scope and method

Arabic qanun practice (Egyptian, Levantine, Iraqi), as distinguished in
`shared/MUSICAL_SYSTEMS/MAQAM.md`. Seven sources were tried; four were read and used at full depth:
MaqamWorld's own qanun page and its instrument-family overview, a University of California Santa
Barbara Middle East Ensemble teaching page, and George Dimitri Sawa's own artist website describing
his reconstruction of an antique, lever-less qanun. Sawa is a practitioner-scholar (a working qanun
player and a published scholar of medieval Arabic music), and his account is the strongest
single-instrument, first-person technical source found for this family. A Michael Frishkopf
ethnomusicology paper was read at full depth for the secular/Sufi ensemble-instrumentation question,
not for qanun construction. An Arabic-language practitioner platform's course-listing page, naming a
named contemporary qanun teacher, was read but is not cited on the page: its content was almost
entirely promotional copy with no extractable technical claim, so it is left out of the register
rather than cited thinly. The two standard texts named in the research spec were not opened in this
pass.

Not reachable: a specialist (non-commercial) source naming the exact lever count of the Arabic qanun,
or comparing it to the Turkish kanun's; a qanun-specific acoustics paper; a qanun-specific
repeated-note or tremolo pedagogy source; region-specific (Egyptian v. Levantine v. Iraqi) construction
or technique pedagogy. Several commercial instrument-seller pages surfaced a specific claim (Arabic
qanuns use four levers per course, Turkish kanuns use more) but disagreed on the exact numbers between
themselves and are not used as sources anywhere in this pass, per the research spec's rule that such
pages may lead to a primary source but are never themselves one.

## Records

### QAN-01 Construction: trapezoidal board, many triple courses, skin-covered resonance bridge
- claim: the qanun is a flat trapezoidal wooden zither with many courses of strings (mostly tripled),
  plucked with two ring-mounted plectra, and its treble-side bridge rests on several taut pieces of
  goat or fish skin covering openings in the soundboard, which is part of its resonance mechanism.
- source: MAQAMWORLD-QANUN (full); UCSB-MEE (full); SAWA-QANUN (full)
- source_type: tradition institution
- confidence: high
- scope: Arabic qanun generally, corroborated by three independently-worded sources, one of them a
  named practitioner's own instrument.
- limitations: none of the three sources gives an acoustic measurement of how much the skin windows
  contribute versus the wooden plate; all three describe the mechanism qualitatively.
- inference: none for the construction facts; the page's framing of the skin-bridge mechanism as
  worth naming precisely follows the sources' own emphasis (UCSB-MEE calls it directly "an important
  part of the instrument's natural amplification system").
- tradition_scope: Arabic qanun practice generally.

### QAN-02 Course count and range are named, disagreeing instances
- claim: three independently-described instances of qanun course count and range disagree: 81
  strings with 24 treble courses of three (MaqamWorld); 25 to 27 sets of strings, mostly triple, over
  three octaves (UCSB); 26 sets of triple strings across three octaves and a fifth, on one named
  antique instrument (Sawa).
- source: MAQAMWORLD-QANUN (full); UCSB-MEE (full); SAWA-QANUN (full)
- source_type: tradition institution; performer
- confidence: low
- scope: three named instances; not a survey and not evidence for one canonical figure.
- limitations: the three figures are not trivially reconcilable (81 strings does not cleanly divide
  into "24 treble courses of three" without further, unstated courses), and no source explains the
  discrepancy or states which is most representative.
- inference: none; the page reports the disagreement rather than resolving it.
- tradition_scope: Arabic qanun practice; the Sawa figure is explicitly one player's one antique
  instrument, not a general claim.

### QAN-03 The lever ('urab) mechanism and the thumbnail-press technique
- claim: small levers under each course, on the instrument's left side, change a course's pitch by a
  fraction of a tone; before and alongside this system, players achieve small temporary pitch changes
  by pressing a course with the left thumbnail; modulating to a new maqam mid-piece requires the left
  hand to operate levers while the right hand continues playing, a real and named phrasing constraint.
- source: MAQAMWORLD-QANUN (full); UCSB-MEE (full); SAWA-QANUN (full)
- source_type: tradition institution; performer
- confidence: high
- scope: Arabic qanun generally; Sawa's account additionally documents the pre-lever, thumbnail-only
  era directly, from having restored and played an instrument built before levers existed.
- limitations: no source gives a magnitude (in seconds) for how long a lever change takes, or a
  measured account of how a modulation is timed against the surrounding phrase; the constraint is
  documented qualitatively, not measured.
- inference: the direct mapping of this mechanism to `shared/TUNING_AND_MPE.md` section 7's
  "retuned by a mechanism during the piece" pitch-behaviour kind is this page's own synthesis of the
  sourced mechanism and the pack's existing framework, not a claim any source makes about the pack.
- tradition_scope: Arabic qanun practice generally.

### QAN-04 Named tuning instances for two maqamat on one antique instrument
- claim: on his own reconstructed, pre-lever antique qanun, the source tunes rast with its third and
  seventh degrees one comma (one-ninth of a tone) flat, and huzam with its fourth degree raised a
  comma and its seventh lowered a comma.
- source: SAWA-QANUN (full)
- source_type: performer
- confidence: medium
- scope: one named performer's tuning of one named antique instrument for two named maqamat; not a
  general cents table for either maqam.
- limitations: no comparison is offered to how a modern, levered qanun would tune the same maqamat;
  the source explicitly frames this instrument as unusual ("an instrument unique in the world").
- inference: none beyond restating the source's own figures; the page does not generalise them.
- tradition_scope: one performer's practice on one named antique instrument, explicitly not
  representative of standard modern qanun tuning by the source's own framing.

### QAN-05 Secular-tarab instrumentation borrowed into one documented Sufi-chant tradition
- claim: in the Egyptian inshad/dhikr tradition documented by this source, ensembles increasingly
  adopted instruments (including, elsewhere in the same literature, instruments of the secular takht)
  while the reed flute retained the primary wind role associated with devotional performance.
- source: FRISHKOPF-MESA-2000 (full)
- source_type: ethnomusicology
- confidence: low
- scope: one ethnomusicologist's account of one documented Egyptian tradition's ensemble evolution
  across the twentieth century.
- limitations: this source discusses inshad ensemble instrumentation generally and the shift toward
  urban-popular instrumentation; it does not single out the qanun by name as clearly as the companion
  source (FRISHKOPF-TARAB-2001, used on the oud page) singles out the oud and violin. The claim as
  used on the qanun page leans on the oud page's better-supported parallel finding and is scoped
  accordingly, at low confidence.
- inference: the page's statement that the qanun is part of a "secular-tarab-derived instrumentation"
  is this pack's extension of the oud-page finding to the qanun, since both are named together as the
  naqr family (`MAQAMWORLD-INSTR`), not a claim FRISHKOPF-MESA-2000 makes about the qanun specifically.
- tradition_scope: Egyptian Sufi/devotional performance practice; not generalised further.
