# Hand Percussion: research records

## Scope and method

This page is deliberately narrow. Its job is to state only what is physically shared across
hand-struck membranes and to route every named tradition to its own page, per the gate in `shared/VIRTUAL_INSTRUMENT_GUIDE/CULTURALLY_SPECIFIC_INSTRUMENTS.md` and its
gate for tradition-specific content (at least two independent specialist sources at section or full
depth, one of them a practitioner, teacher, tradition institution, or ethnomusicological source). No
attempt was made to clear that gate for any of the nine tradition rows in the routing table; doing so
for even one of them would be a separate, substantial piece of work belonging to whichever page ends
up covering that tradition, not to this frame page. The one substantive claim this page does make,
that strike position changes which membrane modes are excited, is general acoustics, not
tradition-specific, and is sourced accordingly.

## Records

### HP-01 Strike position changes which membrane modes are excited
- claim: striking a stretched membrane nearer its centre favours the lowest, most axisymmetric
  vibration mode (low, round, low-overtone); striking nearer the rim excites a richer mix of higher,
  non-axisymmetric modes (brighter, more complex).
- source: WOODHOUSE-TUNED-DRUMS (section)
- source_type: academic acoustics
- confidence: medium
- scope: the source discusses timpani specifically; this page extends the claim to hand-struck
  membranes generally on physical, not tradition-specific, grounds
- limitations: high confidence for the physical mechanism as stated for the source's own subject
  (timpani); rated medium overall for the generalisation to any hand-struck membrane, since the
  underlying physics (mode shape as a function of strike position on a circular membrane) does not
  depend on what excites the membrane or which tradition plays it, but this was not independently
  confirmed for a hand strike. The source does not discuss hand drums, does not give a measured optimum strike
  position for any hand drum, and does not address the additional complexity a hand's compliant,
  multi-point contact area introduces compared with a rigid mallet or stick tip. The generalisation
  from "where a mallet or stick strikes" to "where a hand strikes" assumes the same mode-excitation
  principle applies to a softer, larger contact area, which is physically reasonable but was not
  independently confirmed for a hand strike specifically.
- inference: applying this timpani-specific finding to hand-struck membranes generally, and to the
  bass-versus-open stroke distinction named across many traditions, is this page's own extrapolation.

### HP-02 Pitch-bending by localised tension
- claim: pressing the heel of the hand or the fingers into a membrane, typically near the rim, locally
  increases tension in that region, which raises the frequency of vibration there and bends the
  perceived pitch upward.
- source: none opened at section depth this pass; this is standard membrane physics (a stretched
  membrane's vibration frequency rises with tension, the same relationship that lets a tuned drum be
  tuned at all) rather than a claim read in a specific source.
- source_type: not applicable (general physics, not a specific citation)
- confidence: low
- scope: general to any stretched membrane; the practice of using this technique deliberately (as
  opposed to it merely being physically possible) is documented in specific hand-drum traditions
  (conga, tabla, and others use forms of it) but this page does not claim which traditions, how they
  name it, or how far they bend pitch with it
- limitations: high confidence for the underlying physical mechanism (well-established acoustics:
  membrane frequency scales with the square root of tension); rated low overall because no specific
  instrument's magnitude was measured or sourced here, and the claim is stated as a mechanism, not a
  measured or tradition-attributed technique
- inference: the entire practical claim ("hand drummers use this to bend pitch") beyond the bare
  physics is this page's inference from general knowledge of hand-drum technique, not a claim
  supported by a source read this pass

## Corrections to the 2.0 page

- **`PERCUSSION.md`'s prior "core three strokes on most hand drums: bass, open, slap" is identified
  and corrected as conga/jembe vocabulary presented as universal.** This page states the physical
  fact that generalises (bass-near-centre versus brighter-near-rim, per HP-01) and explicitly declines
  to offer a substitute universal three-stroke (or any-number-of-strokes) vocabulary, routing instead
  to each tradition's own page for its actual stroke names and technique.
- This page did not exist in the 2.0 pack; it is new, per the brief, precisely to hold this
  correction and the tradition-routing table rather than leaving hand percussion folded into
  `PERCUSSION.md`'s orchestral-percussion scope.
