# Nay: research records

What was read, what was not, and the limits of each claim the guide page makes. The page carries
the conclusions; this file carries the evidence.

## Scope and method

Arabic nay practice (Egyptian, Levantine, Iraqi), as distinguished in
`shared/MUSICAL_SYSTEMS/MAQAM.md`. Seven sources were tried; five were read and used at full depth:
MaqamWorld's own nay page and its instrument-family overview, a University of California Santa
Barbara Middle East Ensemble teaching page, and two Michael Frishkopf ethnomusicology papers on
Egyptian Sufi/devotional performance. For the nay, unlike the oud and qanun, the Frishkopf material is
directly on-topic rather than a contrast case, because the nay carries a documented devotional
association the other two instruments do not. An Arabic-language practitioner-platform course listing,
naming a contemporary nay teacher, was read but is not cited on the page: its content was almost
entirely promotional copy with no extractable technical claim. The two standard texts named in the
research spec were not opened in this pass.

Not reachable: a nay-specific acoustics paper (edge-tone mechanism, embouchure-pitch relationship
measured); a nay-specific repeated-note, tonguing or vibrato pedagogy source; a measured breath-phrase
length; region-specific (Egyptian v. Levantine v. Iraqi) construction or technique pedagogy.

## Records

### NAY-01 Construction: open cane tube, nine segments, no reed or mouthpiece
- claim: the Arabic nay is an open-ended cane tube with no reed, fipple or mouthpiece, blown
  obliquely across the bare rim; it has nine segments (formed from a length of cane with eight natural
  nodes), six finger holes on the front and one thumb hole underneath.
- source: MAQAMWORLD-NAY (full); UCSB-MEE (full)
- source_type: tradition institution
- confidence: high
- scope: Arabic nay generally, corroborated by two independently-worded institutional sources.
- limitations: neither source gives a bore diameter, wall thickness, or taper measurement; both are
  descriptive rather than lutherie-technical accounts. UCSB-MEE notes the nine-segment/eight-node
  construction is believed to have "specific, yet unarticulated, acoustic and symbolic functions,"
  which this page reports as an open question rather than resolving.
- inference: none for the construction facts themselves.
- tradition_scope: Arabic nay practice generally.

### NAY-02 A player's range is covered by a set of differently-pitched nays, not one instrument
- claim: each individual nay is built for and named after one pitch (the note sounded with the first
  fingerhole open); the most commonly used nay is named for D4, with a lowest note of C4; a single
  player uses six or seven nays of different lengths to cover a piece's pitch needs.
- source: MAQAMWORLD-NAY (full); UCSB-MEE (full)
- source_type: tradition institution
- confidence: high
- scope: Arabic nay practice generally; both sources agree on the instrument-switching practice
  independently.
- limitations: neither source gives the full set of standard nay pitches or their exact intervallic
  relationship to each other, only that a set exists and is used.
- inference: the programming consequence (treat a wide-ranging part as several instrument changes) is
  this page's synthesis of the sourced practice.
- tradition_scope: Arabic nay practice generally.

### NAY-03 Ensemble role: sahb family, sole wind instrument of the art-music takht
- claim: the nay belongs to the sahb ("pulling or stretching") family with the violin, set against
  the naqr family (oud, qanun); it is the only wind instrument used in Arab art music, inside the
  heterophonic takht texture.
- source: MAQAMWORLD-INSTR (full); MAQAMWORLD-NAY (full)
- source_type: tradition institution
- confidence: high
- scope: the standard Arabic takht ensemble as described by MaqamWorld.
- limitations: neither source explains why the sahb/naqr distinction places a blown instrument with a
  bowed one rather than in a category of its own; the grouping is reported as given, not explained.
- inference: none beyond the naqr-parallel already drawn on the oud and qanun pages.
- tradition_scope: Arabic takht practice generally.

### NAY-04 The nay carries a documented literary/religious association, distinct from the oud's and qanun's secular framing
- claim: a university teaching source records that the nay has genuine "philosophical/mystical
  associations," citing the opening lines of Rumi's *Mathnawi*, where the hollow reed is likened to
  the human body and its sound is said to express longing for union with God; independently, the
  ethnomusicological sources describe reed flutes (nay, kawala) as instruments that are traditional in
  at least one documented Egyptian Sufi-chant (inshad) ensemble tradition, unlike the oud and violin.
- source: UCSB-MEE (full); FRISHKOPF-TARAB-2001 (full); FRISHKOPF-MESA-2000 (full)
- source_type: tradition institution; ethnomusicology
- confidence: medium
- scope: one literary/religious association recorded by a teaching source, and one ethnomusicologist's
  account of documented Egyptian Sufi ensembles; not a claim that every nay performance, or every
  regional practice, is devotional.
- limitations: UCSB-MEE's Rumi reference is a literary/historical association, not a claim about
  present-day performance practice; the Frishkopf sources document one national tradition
  (Egypt) and do not survey Levantine or Iraqi practice. The page is explicit that these are two
  distinct kinds of finding (literary association; documented ensemble practice), not merged into one
  overstated claim.
- inference: the page's recommendation to apply extra caution to nay material specifically, beyond the
  tradition-general caution already in `shared/MUSICAL_SYSTEMS/MAQAM.md`, is this pack's own
  risk-weighted synthesis of the two findings, not a instruction either source states.
- tradition_scope: the Rumi association is pan-Near-Eastern literary tradition, not Arabic-specific;
  the ensemble-practice finding is scoped to documented Egyptian Sufi-chant tradition specifically.
