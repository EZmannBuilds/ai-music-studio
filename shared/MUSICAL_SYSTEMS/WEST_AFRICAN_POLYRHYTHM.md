# Moved: Ewe dance-drumming and Mande jembe music

This file used to hold both Ewe dance-drumming (Ghana, Togo) and Mande jembe music (Mali, Guinea) as
one entry, under a continent used as a genre. `shared/MUSICAL_SYSTEMS/INDEX.md` rule 2 forbids
exactly that. It is split, and this file is kept only so existing links resolve.

**Ewe dance-drumming moved to `shared/MUSICAL_SYSTEMS/EWE_DANCE_DRUMMING.md`.** Instrument
construction and playing technique (gankogui, axatse, kagan, kidi, sogo, atsimevu) moved to
`shared/VIRTUAL_INSTRUMENT_GUIDE/EWE_DANCE_DRUMS.md`.

**Mande jembe music moved to `shared/MUSICAL_SYSTEMS/MANDE_JEMBE_MUSIC.md`.** Instrument
construction and playing technique (jembe, dunun) moved to
`shared/VIRTUAL_INSTRUMENT_GUIDE/JEMBE_AND_DUNUN.md`.

Each new pair carries its own cultural context, sources, and restricted-repertoire notes; nothing
here should be treated as current. The old "four-against-six" and non-isochrony material is now
attributed and scoped separately per tradition in the two new system files.

**This split does not cover the region.** Other West African drumming and ensemble traditions,
including the Dagbamba of northern Ghana, Yoruba drumming (including dùndún) of Nigeria, and Wolof
sabar of Senegal, are distinct traditions in their own right and are not documented anywhere in this
pack. Their absence is a gap in this pack's sources, not a judgement that they matter less; see
`shared/VIRTUAL_INSTRUMENT_GUIDE/CULTURALLY_SPECIFIC_INSTRUMENTS.md` for the protocol a future pass
on any of them would need to pass.
