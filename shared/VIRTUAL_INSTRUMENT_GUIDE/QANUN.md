# Qanun

Traditions: Arabic maqam practice (Egyptian, Levantine, Iraqi). Context:
`shared/MUSICAL_SYSTEMS/MAQAM.md`. The qanun is also central to Turkish makam practice, where it is
called kanun and is widely reported to use a different, finer lever system than the Arabic instrument;
that comparison could not be verified against a specialist source in this pass (see "What must not be
generalised outside the tradition"), and Turkish practice is not covered here.

A plucked trapezoidal box zither, played flat across the lap or a table with two finger-worn plectra,
whose defining mechanism is a bank of small levers that retune each course of strings by a fraction of
a tone during a performance. This page covers Arabic practice only.

> Evidence: seven sources read for this pass, four at full depth: two tradition-institution pages
> (MaqamWorld's qanun page and its instrument-family overview), a university Arab-music-ensemble
> teaching page, and a practitioner-scholar's own account of reconstructing and playing an antique,
> lever-less qanun. An Arabic-language practitioner-platform course listing was read only as a short
> promotional page (excerpt depth), and the two standard reference texts named in the research spec
> were not opened in this pass. Source IDs resolve in `research/sources/INSTRUMENT_SOURCES.md`; the
> claims and their limits are recorded in `research/instruments/QANUN.md`.

Cross-family failures are in `COMMON_ERRORS.md`.

## Tradition and context

### Construction

A flat trapezoidal wooden soundboard, played across the lap or a small table, strung with many
courses of strings running its full width, most courses tripled; a long bridge on the treble side
rests on several taut pieces of goat or fish skin covering openings in the soundboard, which carries
string vibration into the body as a defining resonance mechanism [sourced: MAQAMWORLD-QANUN; UCSB-MEE; SAWA-QANUN]. One practitioner's antique, pre-lever instrument gives exact outer and
string-vibrating dimensions and names its individual string sources (hand-me-down bass strings from a
teacher's workshop, guitar strings for two notes, harp strings for the rest), which is one named
instance of qanun-building practice, not a general specification [sourced: SAWA-QANUN].

### Tuning

**The lever mechanism, precisely, because `shared/TUNING_AND_MPE.md` section 7 needs it stated
exactly.** A bank of small levers (called `'urab` in the sources read here) sits under each course on
the left side of the instrument. Flipping a lever shortens or lengthens the speaking length of that
course's strings by a small, fixed amount, changing its pitch by a fraction of a whole tone
[sourced: MAQAMWORLD-QANUN; UCSB-MEE]. Before this system existed, and still as a documented technique
after it, a player achieves the same kind of small pitch change by pressing a course with the left
thumbnail, which temporarily raises its tuning without touching a lever [sourced: MAQAMWORLD-QANUN; UCSB-MEE; SAWA-QANUN]. Because the instrument starts a piece with its levers set for the tuning of the
opening maqam, modulating to a different maqam mid-piece means the left hand must "switch some levers
back and forth... while playing with the right hand": a real, timed, two-handed constraint, not an
instantaneous free retuning [sourced: MAQAMWORLD-QANUN]. One practitioner-scholar's account gives
named tuning instances for two specific maqamat on one specific (pre-lever) antique instrument: rast
tuned with the third and seventh degrees a comma (one-ninth of a tone) flat, and huzam tuned with its
fourth degree raised a comma and its seventh lowered a comma. These are that one player's tuning of
that one instrument for those two maqamat, not a general table [sourced: SAWA-QANUN].

**Arabic-versus-Turkish lever count was explicitly flagged for verification in the research brief for
this page, and this pass could not settle it.** Commercial and instrument-seller pages disagreed with
each other on the exact numbers (four per note in one account, a range as high as seven to twelve in
another) and are exactly the kind of source this pack's research rules treat as unreliable
alone, so none of them is used here [to-verify: a specialist Arabic and a specialist Turkish source,
each read at section depth, naming their instrument's lever count].

### Note production and technique

Two-handed independent plucking with finger-worn plectra is the basic technique
[sourced: MAQAMWORLD-QANUN; UCSB-MEE]. Pitch-shading technique is the thumbnail press described above,
used for small, quick, temporary changes distinct from a lever flip [sourced: MAQAMWORLD-QANUN; UCSB-MEE; SAWA-QANUN]. This pass found no specialist source describing a qanun tremolo or
repeated-note technique, so none is asserted [to-verify: repeated-note/tremolo technique].

### Idiomatic phrasing and ornamentation

Ornament in the broader tradition belongs to the jins in play, per `shared/MUSICAL_SYSTEMS/MAQAM.md`,
which this page does not repeat. No qanun-specific ornament vocabulary beyond the thumbnail-press
pitch shading already described was found in this pass [inference].

### Performer interaction and ensemble role

Naqr family, alongside the oud, inside a heterophonic takht texture, per the tradition's general
account [sourced: MAQAMWORLD-INSTR]. One source's etymology suggests a tuning-reference role for the
ensemble ("the instrument that lays down the law of pitch for other instruments and singers")
[sourced: MAQAMWORLD-QANUN].

### Repertoire contexts

Takht ensemble accompaniment and song accompaniment, and taqsim (solo instrumental improvisation) as a
recital and recording genre, with named historical exponents recorded by the tradition's own reference
literature [sourced: MAQAMWORLD-QANUN].

### Improvisation

Qanun taqsim follows the same sayr-based structure `shared/MUSICAL_SYSTEMS/MAQAM.md` describes for the
tradition generally. This pass found nothing qanun-specific to add to that general account beyond the
lever/modulation constraint already described under Tuning, which shapes *where* a taqsim can
modulate to without a pause, a genuinely instrument-specific fact [sourced: MAQAMWORLD-QANUN].

## What virtual implementations commonly get wrong

The clearest, best-sourced failure for this instrument is retuning a course silently and instantly, as
if the lever mechanism did not exist: the sources describe modulation as a real, two-handed, timed act,
not a free parameter change [sourced: MAQAMWORLD-QANUN]. A second, related failure is using a
continuous pitch-bend glide for the qanun's variable note, which is the correct mechanism for the
fretless oud and the wrong one here, where pitch is fixed between discrete lever or thumbnail events
[sourced: MAQAMWORLD-QANUN; UCSB-MEE; SAWA-QANUN]. A third, not directly stated by a source but
consistent with the two-hands-free construction: writing the qanun chordally because it physically
can play more than one note at once, which contradicts its documented heterophonic ensemble role
[inference].

## What must not be generalised outside the tradition

Do not treat the lever counts, course counts or ranges given here as universal: three named instances
in this page's own sources already disagree with each other (`practical_range`)
[sourced: MAQAMWORLD-QANUN; UCSB-MEE; SAWA-QANUN]. Do not assume the Turkish kanun uses the same lever
system or count as the Arabic qanun; this pass found the claim that they differ repeated across
unreliable commercial sources but could not confirm it against a specialist source, so neither the
claim nor its specifics are asserted here [to-verify: what would settle this is not recorded]. Do not generalise Egyptian, Levantine and Iraqi
practice as identical; this pass found no source distinguishing them at the construction or technique
level, only at the tuning-instance level already covered in `shared/MUSICAL_SYSTEMS/MAQAM.md`
[to-verify: region-specific qanun pedagogy or performer sources].

## Restricted and ceremonial repertoire

Under `shared/MUSICAL_SYSTEMS/INDEX.md` rule 5, applied per `MAQAM.md`'s sacred-genre caution:
recitation, the call to prayer, Sufi ritual and Eastern Christian chant are not source material. The
ethnomusicological sources read for this pass describe the qanun, like the oud, as part of a
*secular*-tarab-derived instrumentation that some Sufi-chant ensembles borrowed, rather than as a
traditionally Sufi instrument in its own right; the reed flute carries the wind role in that
documented tradition instead [academic: FRISHKOPF-MESA-2000]. That is one ethnomusicologist's account
of one documented Egyptian tradition, not a claim that the qanun is never used devotionally anywhere,
and it is reported at that scope. The adjacent secular repertoire the studio offers instead is what
the rest of this page describes: takht ensemble music, song accompaniment, and taqsim
[sourced: MAQAMWORLD-QANUN].

## What the instrument is

A plucked box zither played flat, whose central programming-relevant fact is a lever mechanism that
retunes courses of strings during a performance, at the cost of a hand and a moment, rather than a
continuously variable pitch (contrast the oud) or a fixed pitch chosen once before playing (contrast
most Western plucked strings), per `shared/TUNING_AND_MPE.md` section 7's general framework, applied
here [sourced: MAQAMWORLD-QANUN; UCSB-MEE].

## Range and register

See `practical_range` above: figures from three named, disagreeing instances span roughly three to
three-and-a-half octaves depending on which instrument is meant [sourced: MAQAMWORLD-QANUN; UCSB-MEE; SAWA-QANUN]. No source read distinguishes register strength or weakness zones for the qanun the way
this guide documents for orchestral instruments, so none is claimed [to-verify: what would settle this is not recorded].

## Articulation and note transitions

```text
two-hand independent plucking       the base technique; each hand its own plectrum, its own course
lever change ('urab / mandal)       discrete, scheduled retuning of a whole course; costs the left hand a moment
thumbnail press                     a brief, one-way pitch-raising nudge on a course, distinct from a lever change
```
[sourced: MAQAMWORLD-QANUN; UCSB-MEE; SAWA-QANUN]

There is no slide, hammer-on or pull-off documented for the qanun in any source read here; its pitch
vocabulary is discrete notes plus the two named retuning mechanisms above, which is a genuinely
different transition logic from a fretted or fretless lute [inference, from the absence of any such
technique in the sources found].

## Physical constraints

```text
many courses (24-27 depending on the named instance), most tripled, laid out across the full board width
two independent plucking hands, but a heterophonic ensemble role, not a chordal one
levers are a shared per-course mechanism: only one pitch per course is available at a time
```
[sourced: MAQAMWORLD-QANUN; UCSB-MEE; SAWA-QANUN]

## Phrase behaviour

Bounded, as with the oud, by what the hands are doing rather than by breath; uniquely among the
instruments in this pack's `shared/MUSICAL_SYSTEMS/MAQAM.md` coverage, a phrase boundary can also be a
*retuning* boundary, since modulating to a new maqam is where a lever change is most likely to happen
[sourced: MAQAMWORLD-QANUN].

## Ensemble behaviour

Naqr family with the oud, heterophonic takht texture, and a possible tuning-reference role for the
ensemble per the etymology note above [sourced: MAQAMWORLD-INSTR; MAQAMWORLD-QANUN].

## Recording behaviour

Played flat, so mic placement is typically from above or from the side, relative to the soundboard and
the skin-covered resonance windows; no source read here described qanun session-recording practice
specifically, so this is inference by analogy with other flat-played strings in this guide [inference].

## Programming it: the control model

Product specifics (controller identities, exact velocity curves) belong in the calibration profile,
not here [inference].

```yaml
velocity: level and, where modelled, plectrum attack strength; not a stand-in for a lever or thumbnail event
lever_changes: discrete, scheduled retuning events per course, per shared/TUNING_AND_MPE.md section 7 tier 3 or a per-course scale change; must occupy a hand and precede the notes that need the new tuning
thumbnail_press: a brief, one-way pitch nudge, modelled separately from a lever change and from vibrato (which the instrument does not have)
course_sharing: only one pitch active per course at a time; never write two simultaneous pitches for one course
```
[sourced: MAQAMWORLD-QANUN]

## Programming it: what makes it sound real

- Treat a maqam modulation as a scheduled retuning event that takes the left hand a moment, not a
  free parameter change with no cost. [sourced: MAQAMWORLD-QANUN]
- Never let a continuous pitch-bend glide stand in for the qanun's variable note; that mechanism
  belongs to the fretless oud, not this instrument. [sourced: MAQAMWORLD-QANUN; UCSB-MEE]
- Keep the texture heterophonic, matching the takht role, rather than writing chordally just because
  two hands are physically free. [sourced: MAQAMWORLD-INSTR]
- Model the thumbnail-press pitch nudge, where used, as brief and one-directional (upward), not as a
  symmetric bend. [sourced: MAQAMWORLD-QANUN]
- Leave plectrum attack noise audible on every note; do not smooth it into a single averaged timbre.
  [inference]

## What sounds fake here

The general list is in `COMMON_ERRORS.md`. Qanun-specific tells:

- a maqam change with no audible or implied retuning moment, as if the levers did not exist;
  [sourced: MAQAMWORLD-QANUN]
- a smooth pitch-bend glide used for the variable note, borrowing the oud's mechanism;
  [sourced: MAQAMWORLD-QANUN; UCSB-MEE]
- chordal writing that ignores the instrument's heterophonic ensemble role; [sourced: MAQAMWORLD-INSTR]
- two simultaneous pitches implied on what should be one physical course; [inference]
- uniform plectrum attack with no per-hand or per-phrase variation. [inference]

## What the Performance Director needs from this file

- A lever change is a `performance_state.timing_character` / phrase-boundary event: schedule it, and
  check that the left hand is free at that moment, per the feasibility logic in
  `shared/HUMAN_PERFORMANCE_SCHEMA.md` section 5.
- `articulation_unavailable`: a general-purpose zither or harp library is unlikely to model the lever
  mechanism or the thumbnail press; report rather than substitute a pedal-harp-style glissando or a
  generic pitch bend.
- `intonation` per `shared/TUNING_AND_MPE.md` section 7 is the right record for a lever's retuned
  value; `pitch_system.degrees_cents` alone does not capture that the value changes mid-piece.
- `impossible_voicings` / `simultaneity_exceeded`: two different pitches on one course at once is not
  physically available; flag it.
- Name the tuning instance in use (from `practical_range` above) in
  `performance_state.performance_reference`, since this page found no single universal qanun tuning.
