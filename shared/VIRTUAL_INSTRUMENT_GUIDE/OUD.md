# Oud

Traditions: Arabic maqam practice (Egyptian, Levantine, Iraqi). Context:
`shared/MUSICAL_SYSTEMS/MAQAM.md`. The oud is also central to Turkish makam practice, and a related
instrument, the barbat, has an ancestor role in Persian dastgah music; both are separate systems this
pack has no `shared/MUSICAL_SYSTEMS/` file for, and neither Turkish nor Persian practice is covered
here.

A fretless, short-necked plucked lute, played with a plectrum (risha), that carries the melodic and
rhythmic lead of the Arabic takht ensemble and is the tradition's principal solo-improvisation
(taqsim) instrument. This page covers Arabic practice only.

> Evidence: nine sources read for this pass, five at full depth: two tradition-institution pages
> written and maintained by practitioners of the tradition (MaqamWorld's oud page, and the
> instrument-family overview that supplies the takht/naqr material), a university Arab-music-ensemble
> teaching page directed by an ethnomusicologist of the tradition, and two ethnomusicology papers on
> the boundary between secular and Sufi/devotional performance. A performer's teaching-blog listing
> was read only as a page of post titles (excerpt depth), and the standard reference texts
> (Farraj & Abu Shumays 2019; Racy 2003) were not opened in this pass and are cited only as such.
> Source IDs resolve in `research/sources/INSTRUMENT_SOURCES.md`; the claims and their limits are
> recorded in `research/instruments/OUD.md`.

Cross-family failures are in `COMMON_ERRORS.md`.

| physical_sound_source | Short-necked, fretless lute: a deep, pear-shaped bowl back built from thin bent wood ribs, a thin wooden soundboard, and a bare (unfretted) neck. Five or six courses of strings, normally paired in unison, plus a single low bass string, are set in motion by a plectrum (risha) held in the right hand; the fretless neck means the left hand stops a string directly against bare wood rather than against a fret | sourced: MAQAMWORLD-OUD; UCSB-MEE |
| attack_behavior | The risha strikes a course in a down- or up-stroke near the bridge/soundhole area. The tradition's own descriptive literature calls the oud "percussive," meaning the attack itself carries rhythmic information and not only pitch onset, which is part of why the instrument can lead a rhythmic accompaniment and a melodic line at once | sourced: MAQAMWORLD-OUD |
| sustain_behavior | Decays from the moment of the pluck, as with any plucked chordophone; there is no true sustain without re-attack or risha tremolo (rapid alternating strokes on one held pitch) | inference |
| release_behavior | The left hand lifts off the fretless neck to end a stopped note; an open (unfingered) course must be damped by the right hand or a resting left-hand finger, since there is no fret edge to define where the string stops ringing | inference |
| dynamic_timbre_change | Because the attack is percussive (see above), loudness and "bite" are coupled to how hard and where the risha strikes. Because the neck is fretless, left-hand finger pressure and position also shift timbre independent of loudness, by the general plucking-point mechanism this pack documents for other plucked strings | sourced: MAQAMWORLD-OUD + inference |
| register_character | Described in the tradition's own literature as having "a warm timbre and a wide tonal range (about 3 octaves)." | practical_range | **Tuning is a named regional choice, not a single fact about "the oud."** One widely used modern tuning, low to high, is C F A D G C, with every interval a perfect fourth apart except F to A. A university Arab-ensemble teaching source gives a differently-spelled reference tuning, GG AA D G c, with a sixth string commonly added (a pair at f, or a single low string at CC or DD), and records that the lowest course is "alternatively tuned by some" a step or two away from its own stated note. | pitch_instability | Intonation is deliberately non-fixed: the fretless neck lets a player place a jins's variable note where the practice requires, a placement that depends on register, direction and which jins is in play rather than a constant cents value. See `shared/MUSICAL_SYSTEMS/MAQAM.md` and `shared/TUNING_AND_MPE.md` section 7, whose "Continuous" pitch-behaviour kind names fretless strings explicitly | inference |
| resonance | Paired unison courses ring together when struck; the pear-shaped bowl body resonates as a whole, which the tradition's literature credits directly for part of the instrument's warm timbre. A course not being played can still be excited sympathetically by a struck course sharing a partial, as documented elsewhere in this guide for other strings in general | sourced: MAQAMWORLD-OUD + inference |
| physical_noise | Risha attack noise (a percussive click as it strikes a course) and left-hand slide noise moving along bare wood, audible in a way a fretted lute's discrete fret clicks are not | inference |
| feasibility | Paired strings are struck together as one course; a course, not a single string, is the smallest addressable pitch. ## Tradition and context

### Construction

The bowl back is built from thin bent wood ribs glued edge to edge into a deep pear shape, over a
thin wooden soundboard, with a short, fretless neck and (typically) a downward-angled pegbox
[sourced: MAQAMWORLD-OUD; UCSB-MEE]. The strings were historically gut or silk; modern instruments
use nylon for the higher courses and metal-wound strings over a silk or nylon core for the lower ones
[sourced: UCSB-MEE]. The risha was historically cut from an eagle feather (the word means "feather")
and is now usually plastic, tortoise shell, animal horn or bamboo [sourced: MAQAMWORLD-OUD; UCSB-MEE].

### Tuning

There is no single tuning; see `practical_range` above for two named, sourced instances that
disagree note for note while sharing the same fourths-based logic, and for the explicit record that
even the lowest course varies by player [sourced: MAQAMWORLD-OUD; UCSB-MEE]. A tuning is set before
the piece; the oud is a "frets that were placed/strings tuned for the music" case in
`shared/TUNING_AND_MPE.md` section 7's fixed-tuning sense, not a during-the-piece retuning
instrument like the qanun [inference, applying that page's framework].

### Note production and technique

The risha provides the attack; the fretless left hand provides both discrete pitches and continuous
motion between them (see `transitions` and `pitch_instability` above) [sourced: MAQAMWORLD-OUD; UCSB-MEE]. A teaching resource by a working performer of the tradition lists structured
position-based fingering study (an etude organised by neck position on one course) as part of how the
instrument is taught, which corroborates that hand position, not fret position, organises technique
here [standard-reference: PRUNKA-WORDS].

### Idiomatic phrasing and ornamentation

Ornament belongs to the jins, not to a generic vocabulary layered on top of it, per
`shared/MUSICAL_SYSTEMS/MAQAM.md`'s ornamentation section, which this page does not repeat
[inference]. On the oud specifically, that principle is realised through slide placement,
vibrato placement, and repeated-note/tremolo figures rather than through a closed list of named
ornaments this research could confirm [inference].

### Performer interaction and ensemble role

Naqr-family instrument, paired against the sahb family, inside a heterophonic takht texture; see
`ensemble_behavior` above [sourced: MAQAMWORLD-INSTR + inference]. The oud is also commonly the
instrument a singer accompanies themself on, and the instrument composers write new melodies on
[sourced: MAQAMWORLD-OUD].

### Repertoire contexts

Song accompaniment, takht ensemble melody, and taqsim (solo instrumental improvisation) are the
three contexts named in the tradition's own descriptive literature, with named historical exponents
of oud taqsim recorded across Egypt, Syria and Palestine [sourced: MAQAMWORLD-OUD].

### Improvisation

Taqsim on the oud follows the sayr of the chosen maqam exactly as `shared/MUSICAL_SYSTEMS/MAQAM.md`
describes for the tradition generally: establish the lower ajnas, dwell, may modulate elsewhere, must
return, and closes with a qafla [inference]. Nothing found in this pass is oud-specific
beyond that general account, so no oud-only addition is made to it [to-verify: an oud-specific
performer or pedagogy source on taqsim structure, beyond the tradition-general account already in
MAQAM.md].

## What virtual implementations commonly get wrong

The two failures named at card level are the ones this pass found stated or strongly implied by the
sources: silent quantisation to twelve-tone equal temperament, which removes the jins-dependent
variable note that the whole tuning section of `shared/MUSICAL_SYSTEMS/MAQAM.md` is built around
[inference], and writing the oud as a harmonic/chordal part, which contradicts both its
paired-course construction and its heterophonic ensemble role [sourced: MAQAMWORLD-OUD; MAQAMWORLD-INSTR]. A third, not directly sourced but consistent with the fretless construction: a
legato patch built and tuned for a fretted instrument (discrete stepped transitions) misrepresents an
instrument whose idiomatic transition is a continuous glide [inference].

## What must not be generalised outside the tradition

Do not treat this page as covering the oud generally. Turkish makam practice and Persian dastgah
(where the related instrument is the barbat) are distinct systems, named as distinct in
`shared/MUSICAL_SYSTEMS/MAQAM.md`'s cultural-context section, with their own theory and repertoire
[inference]. Do not generalise a claim from one named tuning (`practical_range` above) to "the"
oud tuning; the sources found here already disagree with each other [sourced: MAQAMWORLD-OUD; UCSB-MEE]. Do not generalise Egyptian, Levantine and Iraqi practice as interchangeable; this pass did
not find region-specific technical detail sufficient to distinguish them beyond the maqam-system-level
distinctions `shared/MUSICAL_SYSTEMS/MAQAM.md` already makes, and that gap is left open rather than
invented [to-verify: region-specific oud pedagogy or performer sources for Egyptian, Levantine and
Iraqi schools separately].

## Restricted and ceremonial repertoire

Under `shared/MUSICAL_SYSTEMS/INDEX.md` rule 5, applied here per `MAQAM.md`'s sacred-genre caution:
recitation, the call to prayer, Sufi ritual and Eastern Christian chant are not source material, and
the studio declines to imitate, sample or set new words to them. For the oud specifically, the
ethnomusicological sources read for this pass describe the oud, together with the violin, as an
instrument of *secular* tarab music that is explicitly "not traditional in Sufi music," where reed
flutes (nay, kawala) carry the wind role instead [academic: FRISHKOPF-TARAB-2001]. That is a claim
about one documented Sufi-chant ensemble tradition in Egypt, not a universal rule that the oud is
never used devotionally elsewhere, and it is reported at that scope. The adjacent secular repertoire
the studio does offer is exactly what the rest of this page describes: takht ensemble music, song
accompaniment, and taqsim [sourced: MAQAMWORLD-OUD; FRISHKOPF-TARAB-2001].

## What the instrument is

A fretless, short-necked lute carrying both the melodic line and, through its percussive risha
attack, a rhythmic function at once, inside an ensemble that has no chordal accompaniment section
[sourced: MAQAMWORLD-OUD]. Its two defining physical facts for programming purposes are that the neck
has no frets, so intonation is continuous and context-dependent rather than a fixed table, and that
strings are struck in paired courses rather than singly, so the instrument's natural voice is one
melodic line, occasionally doubled, never a full chord [sourced: MAQAMWORLD-OUD; UCSB-MEE].

## Range and register

See `practical_range` in the card: two named, sourced tunings disagree on their exact notes while
agreeing on the fourths-based, five-or-six-course-plus-bass shape [sourced: MAQAMWORLD-OUD; UCSB-MEE]. Total compass across both cited tunings is roughly three octaves from the lowest bass
course [sourced: MAQAMWORLD-OUD]. Register character (warm low end used as a drone/pedal, brighter
top course for contrast) is inference extending the "wide tonal range" description in the source
literature, not a claim the source states in those terms [sourced: MAQAMWORLD-OUD + inference].

## Articulation and note transitions

```text
risha down-stroke / up-stroke   the basic attack; direction is a phrasing choice, as on other plectrum instruments
risha tremolo                   rapid stroke alternation on one held pitch; regularity is not confirmed for the oud specifically
hammer-on / pull-off            discrete left-hand legato, quieter than a picked note before it, by the general plucked-lute mechanism
slide / glissando               continuous, any interval size including microtonal, unique to the fretless construction
left-hand vibrato                rocking pressure at any neck position; jins-dependent, not constant
```
[sourced: MAQAMWORLD-OUD; UCSB-MEE + inference]

The slide row is the one that most separates the oud from a fretted lute in this guide: on a fretted
instrument a "slide" moves between two defined fret positions, and on the oud it is genuinely
continuous, which is the mechanism `shared/TUNING_AND_MPE.md` section 7 calls out by naming fretless
strings under its "Continuous" pitch-behaviour kind [inference].

## Physical constraints

```text
five or six courses, paired strings struck together as one course, plus a single bass string
no frets: left-hand position is continuous, not stepped
one melodic line (occasionally lightly doubled by an open course), not chords
```
[sourced: MAQAMWORLD-OUD; UCSB-MEE]

The instrument's own construction argues against writing it chordally: courses are paired for unison
doubling, not for independent harmony notes, and the ensemble role documented above is heterophonic
melody, not harmonic accompaniment [sourced: MAQAMWORLD-OUD; MAQAMWORLD-INSTR + inference].

## Phrase behaviour

Bounded by hand position, not breath. A taqsim's phrase architecture is the sayr of the chosen
maqam, described generally in `shared/MUSICAL_SYSTEMS/MAQAM.md`: establish the lower ajnas, dwell on
the tonic and ghammaz, permit modulation elsewhere, and close each section with a qafla
[inference]. This research did not find an oud-specific refinement of that general account
(a typical taqsim length, a named oud-specific phrase convention), and none is invented here
[to-verify: an oud-specific performer or pedagogy source on taqsim phrase length and structure].

## Ensemble behaviour

Naqr family (with the qanun), heterophonic takht texture, common song-accompaniment and taqsim
instrument; see `ensemble_behavior` in the card, sourced there [sourced: MAQAMWORLD-INSTR; MAQAMWORLD-OUD + inference]. Two ouds playing the same line in the same register mask each other in the way this
guide documents generally for unison melodic instruments; the takht's usual solution, register and
timbral variety across different instruments playing the same line, is intrinsic to heterophony rather
than a separate fix [inference].

## Recording behaviour

Mic distance trades risha attack and bowl resonance against room blend, as in `recording_behavior`
above [inference]. Nothing oud-specific beyond general close/distant miking practice was found or is
claimed here [to-verify: a recording-engineering source specific to oud sessions in this tradition].

## Programming it: the control model

Product specifics (controller identities, exact velocity curves, patch names) belong in the
calibration profile, not here [inference].

```yaml
velocity: level and, where modelled, risha attack character; not a stand-in for pitch placement
articulation_switching: keyswitches or an articulation list, most likely, for tremolo/hammer-pull/slide
legato_patches: monophonic per course; need overlap to trigger a transition sample
fretless_pitch: needs a genuine per-note pitch/modulation lane (TUNING_AND_MPE.md tier 3), not a scale file
course_doubling: unison pairs, not independent voices; do not use a course to carry a second harmony note
```
[inference]

## Programming it: what makes it sound real

- Place the variable note of the active jins by ear and by the maqam's sayr, not from a fixed scale
  file; use per-note pitch control. [inference]
- Write one heterophonic line for the ensemble to decorate, not chords under it.
  [sourced: MAQAMWORLD-INSTR + inference]
- Let slides be genuinely continuous, including microtonal ones, rather than stepped between fixed
  pitches. [inference]
- Give risha tremolo a named cause (stroke alternation, per
  `shared/HUMAN_PERFORMANCE_SCHEMA.md`) for any timing or level variation, not unmodelled
  randomisation. [inference]
- Leave risha attack noise and left-hand slide noise audible; do not strip incidental noise from a
  percussive, fretless instrument. [inference]
- Shape a taqsim by the sayr (establish, dwell, modulate, return, close on a qafla), not as a
  free-form solo with no destination. [inference]

## What sounds fake here

The general list is in `COMMON_ERRORS.md`. Oud-specific tells:

- every note locked to twelve-tone equal temperament, erasing the jins's variable note;
  [inference]
- chords or harmonic accompaniment written for the oud inside a takht texture;
  [sourced: MAQAMWORLD-INSTR + inference]
- a fretted-instrument legato patch substituted for the fretless slide, so transitions move in fixed
  steps instead of continuously; [inference]
- uniform risha attack with no tremolo, slide or vibrato irregularity anywhere; [inference]
- silence where risha or left-hand slide noise should be. [inference]

## What the Performance Director needs from this file

- `impossible_voicings` / `simultaneity_exceeded`: chordal writing beyond one doubled unison course
  is not idiomatic; flag it rather than passing it through silently, even though nothing here makes
  it physically impossible on a sampled instrument. [sourced: MAQAMWORLD-OUD + inference]
- `out_of_range`: use the range implied by whichever named tuning (`practical_range`) the part
  declares; do not assume a single universal range.
- `articulation_unavailable`: risha tremolo, fretless slides of arbitrary size, and jins-dependent
  vibrato are the three articulations most likely to be missing from a general-purpose plucked-string
  library; report rather than substitute a fretted-lute equivalent. [inference]
- Tuning is a `performance_state.performance_reference` decision
  (`shared/HUMAN_PERFORMANCE_SCHEMA.md`): name the tuning used, from `practical_range` above, in the
  plan.
- `intonation` per `shared/TUNING_AND_MPE.md` section 7 is the correct record for the variable note,
  not `pitch_system.degrees_cents` alone; the placement is phrase- and jins-dependent.
